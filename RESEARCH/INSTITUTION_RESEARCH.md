## Verified history (Entity A — shared heritage)

- **Founding:** Established 1912 (the date over the main gate) by pearl merchant **Sheikh Ahmed bin Dalmouk bin Majid Al Falasi**. He died before completion. His son **Sheikh Mohammed bin Ahmed bin Dalmouk** finished it and named it after his father. Described as Dubai's first semi-formal private (ahliya) school.
  Sources: dubaiculture.gov.ae; ar.wikipedia; Gulf News.
- **Disputed founding date:** ~1905, per former student and author Abdul Ghaffar Hussain (Gulf News).
- **Building:** Built in phases, 1912–1922. Two storeys, ~528 m². Courtyard, classrooms, teachers' residence, wind tower, teak gate with brass studs.
  Sources: Gulf News; ar.wikipedia (the latter flagged as needing sources).
- **Curriculum:**
  - Early teaching covered Qur'an, Hadith, Seerah, Tawhid, Arabic, and arithmetic. Teachers came from Al-Ahsa and Az-Zubair.
  - It later added literature, sciences, history and geography.
  - It became one of the first regular (formal) schools when formal education began in Dubai in 1956.
  Sources: ar.wikipedia; Insight Guides; Al Khaleej.
- **Notable pupil:** Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum is pictured at a 1957 student festival there (WAM via The National). The Arabic Wikipedia claim that he "attended from 1955" is unsourced and should not be used.
- **Closure:** Moved to a new building in 1962/63 (sources differ). More than 3,500 pupils attended before closure (Gulf News).
- **Museum:** Restored from 1994. Inaugurated as a museum by Sheikh Hamdan bin Rashid Al Maktoum on 20 March 2000.
- **Centenary (2012):** Celebrated with H.H. Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum attending (Khaleej Times; The National; Emarat Al Youm 5 Sep 2012).
- **Commemoration:** CBUAE silver coin; Emirates Post stamp issue.
